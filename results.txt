### Step 1 — Mode distinction: PARTIAL (`partial_primary_unreplicated`)

Purpose:
- Test whether the token windows labeled “prose” and “tool call” correspond to distinguishable model execution contexts.
- This is a validation gate for the window definitions, not the main research hypothesis.

Input:
- Mind the GAP and τ-Bench agent trajectories containing assistant prose and structured tool calls.
- Token-level prose/tool windows.
- Residual-stream activations projected through the model’s output vocabulary at each tested layer.

Output:
- Mean vocabulary entropy for prose and tool-call windows.
- Paired entropy difference Δ = tool entropy − prose entropy.
- Effect size, raw p-value, Bonferroni p-value, and BH-FDR p-value.
- Cross-dataset replication status at the same layer.

Expected result:
- Tool-call entropy should be lower than prose entropy, so Δ<0.
- The preregistered final-layer test should have p<0.05 on both GAP and τ-Bench.
- If only the exploratory sweep succeeds, the same layer must have Δ<0 and BH-FDR p<0.05 independently in both datasets.

For an individual test, PASS requires both (1) tool entropy lower than prose
(Δ<0) and (2) p<0.05, following the preregistered α=0.05 threshold (preregistration.md).
For an exploratory layer sweep, the corrected p-value
must be <0.05. The full Step 1 gate is validated only if the result also
replicates at the same layer in both GAP and τ-Bench.

### Step 3 — Refusal-direction stability: SUGGESTIVE, SCALE-CONFOUNDED

Purpose:
- Test whether an Arditi-style refusal direction extracted from prose remains stable when extracted from tool-call contexts.
- Separate a mode-specific failure from general extraction noise by comparing against a prose↔prose split-half baseline.

Input:
- Harmful-versus-harmless contrast pairs for prose extraction.
- Matched teacher-forced tool-call contrast pairs.
- Residual-stream activations at fixed early, middle, and late layers.

Output:
- A refusal direction for prose and a separate refusal direction for tool-call mode at each layer.
- Prose↔prose split-half cosine and prose↔tool cosine.
- Layer-matched gap = prose↔prose cosine − prose↔tool cosine.
- Joint BCa 95% confidence intervals and depth-grid stability status.

Expected result:
- A reliable same-mode refusal direction should have high prose↔prose cosine.
- If tool mode destabilizes the direction, prose↔tool cosine should be lower than the same-layer prose↔prose baseline.
- On Qwen3-32B, the fixed stability gate passes only if at least 3 of 4 preregistered mid/late layers have prose↔tool cosine≥0.70.
- Exploratory depth effects are corrected with BH-FDR within Step 3 and cannot change the fixed gate.

- Same-mode prose split-half baseline:
  - L4≈0.36, L14≈0.70, L22≈0.73.
- Prose↔tool point estimates:
  - L4≈0.36, L14≈0.31, L22≈0.16.
- Joint BCa gap intervals:
  - L4: [−0.23, 0.46] → inconclusive/shared floor.
  - L14: [−0.04, 0.35] → inconclusive.
  - L22: [0.42, 0.56] → excludes zero; late-layer gap is suggestive.
- The joint BCa re-run materially changed the conclusion. The earlier conditional percentile interval made L14 appear resolved ([0.36, 0.66]); the joint interval includes zero. The accurate framing is therefore: L22 shows a suggestive late-layer gap, L14 is unresolved, and L4 is floor—not “the mid/late gap is robust.”
- BCa acceleration and bias-correction estimates are themselves unstable with single-digit tool samples. These intervals are directionally informative, not tightly calibrated. The 32B run must check whether the BCa diagnostics stabilize at larger n.
- Frozen 32B stability gate: fixed mid/late set `F_mid={0.50, 0.60, 0.70, 0.80}`; PASS requires at least 3 of 4 layers to have prose↔tool cosine≥0.70. Early/final layers cannot rescue a failed majority.
- Exploratory depth-grid gap tests form their own §3 BH-FDR family and are not pooled with §1.
- Canonical result: `data/results/refusal_stability_layer_matched.json`.

The “majority rule” is operationalized over the four preregistered grid layers,
not bootstrap resamples: at least three of the four must meet the cosine
threshold. The fixed stability gate has no multiplicity correction; exploratory
depth trends are corrected separately within §3.

Connection to Step 4:
- Step 3 measures a safety-relevant direction, but a prose/tool difference could reflect general mode brittleness rather than safety-specific instability.
- Step 4 constructs random orthogonal and task-relevant non-safety control directions.
- Before a learned syntax or domain-content control can normalize Step 5, it must pass the same prose↔prose split-half reliability check: at least 3 of 4 fixed mid/late layers must have cosine≥0.70, with joint BCa intervals reported.
- If a learned control is unstable, it must be rebuilt or excluded; otherwise a safety-versus-control difference would be confounded by extraction noise on both sides.
- Random orthogonal controls are sampled rather than extracted, so they do not use split-half stability. Their seed, norm, and orthogonality must instead be verified.
- Later perturbation tests compare refusal, Assistant Axis, and harmlessness directions against these controls. A safety-specific claim is supported only if safety directions change more than the controls under the same prose→tool transition.

### What is solidly closed (claim language)
**At L4:** persona-space compression is driven by the **behavioral act of tool-call emission**, not by Hermes format alone and not by emit-instruction phrasing alone. Causal chain supported: lookup nudge → actual `<tool_call>` emission → rank collapse. D3 is the decisive piece (same prompt/nudge; only emission differs). ### Scope limits (must appear in any write-up body, not only footnotes)

1. **L4 finding, not a whole-network finding.** D3 CIs touch at L14/L22, so emission→compression is cleanly isolated only at early layer L4. Do **not** write “the model” / “persona representations” without a layer qualifier. **Decision (locked):** use the **31-dim prose-mode PCA basis** (all components available from the 32-role prose matrix; primary readout layers as in Phase 1) as the **canonical storage/interpretation basis** for directions going into Phase 2/3. Do **not** adopt the collapsed tool-prompted subspace (~8-dim @90%) as canonical — that compression is what the causal protocol attributed to tool-call emission under lookup prompting, not to a stable “tool-mode persona manifold” we should align to.

## Statistical threshold reference

The p<0.05 cutoff is a predeclared decision threshold, not proof that an
effect is real or important. The conventional 5% level traces to Fisher
(1925), while modern guidance warns against interpreting it as a bright line:

- Fisher, R. A. (1925). Statistical Methods for Research Workers. Edinburgh: Oliver & Boyd.
- Wasserstein, R. L., & Lazar, N. A. (2016). “The ASA Statement on p-Values: Context, Process, and Purpose.” The American Statistician, 70(2), 129–133. https://doi.org/10.1080/00031305.2016.1154108

Here, α=0.05 is used because it was frozen in the project preregistration before
the confirmatory analysis. Effect direction, effect size, uncertainty,
multiplicity correction, and cross-dataset replication are reported separately.

## §4 — mostly solid, one thing to double check

The gate design is right (learned controls need the same reliability bar as the thing they normalize). The therapy v1→v2 fix (2/4 fail → expand pairs → 4/4 pass) is handled correctly and transparently — documented as an amendment, not silently replaced.

One thing worth flagging: **domain-content and syntax controls were validated at L14/L16/L19/L22, but §5 re-extracted them at L4** ("learned controls re-extracted at L4 from Step-4 pairs"). The split-half stability check never ran at L4 — it was only checked at the four mid/late layers. This is a subtle version of the same confound §3 caught: you're trusting the control's *stability* based on evidence from different layers than the ones doing the work. Worth running the split-half check at L4 specifically (or wherever perturbation is actually injected) before treating the Step 5 controls as validated.

## §5 — the real content, and the chart tells a sharper story than the prose alone

This is the important step, and the analysis is honest, but a few things deserve more scrutiny than "null, don't claim RQ1" leaves on the table.

**1. The interaction is driven entirely by random controls going *negative*, not by safety directions going up.**
Look at the numbers: safety (excl. refusal) Δ≈+1.18, learned controls Δ≈+1.15 — these are nearly identical, hence the null learned-control interaction (+0.02). But random controls average Δ≈−2.20, which is what inflates the *pooled* interaction to +1.70. A −2.20 mean for random directions is itself a strange result worth interrogating rather than just excluding from the headline claim: why would unit-norm random orthogonal directions become *more* geometrically privileged (lower ε*, i.e., harder to perturb) in tool mode than in prose? That's not "no effect," that's a specific, unexplained directional signal in the opposite direction from the hypothesis. It could be a real property of tool-call activation geometry (e.g., tool windows are lower-entropy/more constrained, so *any* random direction becomes locally more stable there) — which would actually support the "globally more brittle/constrained" concern the original methodology flagged as needing normalization. But it's currently just noted as "they drive pooled interaction," not investigated. Given it's CI95 [1.37, 2.39] and excludes zero, this is not noise — it's a real, unexplained effect that the write-up correctly avoids overclaiming with (good), but doesn't yet explain.

**2. Individual safety-direction results span a huge range and are being averaged over.**
Δ for the three safety directions (relative): refusal ≈+4.07 (blocked, unreliable per §3), harmlessness ≈+2.63, Assistant Axis ≈−0.28. Averaging harmlessness and Assistant Axis to get "+1.18" hides that these two move in *opposite directions* under relative. The central prediction named Assistant Axis deprivilege in tool mode — relative −0.28 looked consistent, but absolute/norm-matched flip it to ≈+1.5 (see 0.6B watch-list close-out). That relative-only support has evaporated; report per-direction under the preferred metrics, not the relative pooled mean.

**3. Domain-content controls also disagree with each other in sign** (coding −1.05ish negative-looking from the image vs. writing ≈+2.7, therapy ≈+1.0), similar magnitude spread to the safety directions. That's useful context: it suggests high per-direction variance is a general feature of this pilot (any category, safety or control), not something specific to safety directions — which if anything strengthens the "null" reading, since safety isn't unusually variable relative to controls.

**4. n is tiny and this is explicitly flagged, which is correct** — 8 transcripts, 24 windows, single directions per category (except 4 random). No CI is reported on the individual safety-direction Δs or the learned-control interaction point estimate (+0.02) itself, only on the pooled random-driven one. Given how close +1.18 and +1.15 are, it would be worth knowing whether the "null" learned-control interaction (+0.02) has a CI comparable in width to the pooled one's ([1.37, 2.39], width≈1) — if so, +0.02 could easily be consistent with anything from meaningfully negative to meaningfully positive, and "null" is currently an eyeballed point-estimate comparison, not a CI-based claim like the pooled result was given.

## Bottom line

The step sequencing and epistemic honesty are good — resisting the pull toward the flashy pooled +1.70 number in favor of the properly normalized null is exactly right, and is the same discipline that caught the L14 BCa overclaim earlier. Before treating §5 as "done, move to §6+" I'd want two things: (a) a CI on the learned-control interaction itself, not just the pooled one, since right now "null" is asserted from a point estimate; and (b) some explanation attempt for the random-control negative shift, since it's a real (CI-excludes-zero) and currently unexplained effect that bears on whether "tool mode is globally more constrained" is true — which matters for how §5 gets designed at 32B regardless of what happens with safety directions specifically.

**Uniform additive shift (why interaction holds while every point estimate moves):**

individual Δ moves. That is mechanical, not coincidental.

**Magnitude check — does ~9% shrinkage predict the ~1.7-unit shift?**

Formula (linear response ||δ(ε)|| ≈ α·ε, mode-independent α):

  ε*_rel ≈ τ · ||h|| / α

  pure-norm contribution to relative Δ:
    (N_tool − N_prose) / N_prose · ε*_prose

  Absolute (shared threshold) has no per-window denom → that contribution ≈ 0.

  Therefore predicted relative → absolute shift:
    (Δ_abs − Δ_rel) ≈ (1 − N_tool/N_prose) · ε*_prose

  Plug in N_tool/N_prose ≈ 0.9106:
    (Δ_abs − Δ_rel) ≈ 0.0894 · ε*_prose

| Aggregate | mean ε*_prose | predicted shift | observed (abs−rel) | residual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| safety mean (excl. refusal) | ~24.8 | **+2.22** | **+1.75** | −0.47 |
| learned mean | ~22.2 | **+1.98** | **+1.69** | −0.29 |
| random mean (canceling) | ~27.0 | **+2.42** | **+2.74** | +0.32 |
| overall (excl. refusal) | — | **+2.20** | **+2.12** | mean \|resid\| **0.51** |

Per-direction random residuals (do **not** fold into “~0.3–0.5 second-order”):

| Direction | predicted shift | observed (abs−rel) | residual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| rand_00 | +2.00 | +2.03 | +0.03 |
| rand_01 | +2.63 | +1.89 | −0.74 |
| rand_02 | +2.33 | **+4.43** | **+2.10** |
| rand_03 | +2.71 | +2.61 | −0.10 |
